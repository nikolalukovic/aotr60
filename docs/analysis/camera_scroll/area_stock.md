# AREA stock

## SUMMARY

The slowdown is stock behaviour of the RotWK/AotR W3DView terrain-follow, not something AotR60 introduced. While you pan with a scroll amount of 50 or more (ScrollAmountCutoff), the engine keeps the eye at a fixed absolute height: zoom is frozen unless the camera drops below 120 above the terrain. But it re-aims the look-at point at the terrain under the camera every frame. In W3DView::update (0x48C1E8-0x48C23D) groundLevel (W3DView+0x2408) is set to the camera height-grid sample at pos, clamped to 5000. buildCameraTransform (0x489F96 -> 0x502858) puts the eye at pos - forward*(E - groundLevel)*cot(37.5 deg) with E = 300*zoom. So when the terrain under the screen centre drops while you scroll forward, the eye's horizontal offset grows and eats into the scroll. The eye (and so the image) moves at v*(1 + 1.303*s), where s is the slope of the camera height grid along the camera's forward direction. On a slope that falls away from the camera, s is negative: about 0.61x at s = -0.3, 0.35x at s = -0.5, and 0.22x at the grid's slope limit of -0.6. Scrolling forward over such a slope means going downhill, and scrolling back over it means going uphill. Both are slow, which matches "either when going down or up it slows down". Slopes facing the camera speed things up in both directions instead (up to 1.65-1.78x). At an Options.ini ScrollFactor of 50 (H = V = 1.0) and KeyboardScrollSpeedFactor 1.0, keyboard scroll (100 per axis, 250 in the InGameUI path), edge scroll after half its 250 ms ramp, and right-mouse drags farther than about 49 px from the anchor are all at or over the cutoff. So normal panning is always in this frozen-eye mode. The scroll speed itself is 0.25*zoom*|scroll amount| world units per 30 Hz step. It does not depend on the terrain slope or the height above ground; it depends only on zoom, which is absolute eye height / 300.

## STOCK

Yes, the slowdown exists at stock 30 FPS (CONFIRMED from code, INFERRED for the exact numbers). W3DView::update moves the look-at point onto the camera height grid while scrolling (groundLevel W3DView+0x2408 updated at 0x48C232). At the same time it holds the eye at a fixed absolute height whenever |scroll amount| >= ScrollAmountCutoff 50 (0x48C309-0x48C357), unless the camera drops below DefaultCameraMinHeight 120 above the grid. The eye sits (E - groundLevel)*cot(37.5 deg) behind the look-at point, so its horizontal speed is v*(1 + 1.303*s), where s is the grid slope along the camera's forward direction (|s| <= about 0.6 after the grid's slope-limiting dilation). Over a slope falling away from the camera, both scrolling forward (downhill) and backward (uphill) slow the image: 0.61x at s = -0.3, 0.35x at -0.5, 0.22x at -0.6. Camera-facing slopes speed it up by up to 1.78x. With ScrollFactor 50 and KeyboardScrollSpeedFactor 1.0, keyboard (|SA| = 100/250), edge after its ramp (100) and right-mouse drags over about 49 px all trigger this frozen-height mode. The original Generals code comment calls the frozen height a deliberate 'non-dizzying' choice; RotWK added the per-frame groundLevel re-aim on top of it, and the two together produce the effect. Expected stopwatch test at 30 FPS (Ctrl+Shift+F11): same slowdown on the back side of a hill in both directions, speed-up on the front side, and much less effect with a small right-mouse drag (under about 49 px).

## AOTR60

Neither causes nor amplifies it (INFERRED from the design and the telemetry). The camera is stepped only on A-renders by the 100 % stock W3DView::update, LookAt translator and scrollBy, so the eye positions M_k are the stock 30 Hz sequence with the speed change built in. SceneOpen_A shows halfway(M_{k-1}, M_k), which keeps that speed profile. B-renders swap to M_k. FOV and pitch are constant, and per-frame eye moves are about 8-41 units (far below 1500), so the CUT rules do not fire while panning; the log shows only 6 cuts in about 6 minutes. The only interaction is perceptual: at 60 FPS with split present the motion is smooth enough that a change of 0.35x-1.7x in eye speed is easier to notice than in stock's choppier 30 FPS. The interpolation adds a constant one-half-step lag (about 17 ms), which does not change speed.

## FIX

No AotR60 fix is needed; this is stock camera design. If the user wants it gone, the options are:
(1) Data only (all modes): in AotR gamedata.ini:11275 set ScrollAmountCutoff above any reachable scroll amount, e.g. 100000. Keyboard is 141 diagonal or 250 in the InGameUI path; right-mouse is about pixel distance, up to about 2000 at 4K. W3DView+0x2404 copies it at view init (0x48BCD8). The eye height then tracks H + terrain during panning at CameraAdjustSpeed 0.3 per frame, so the eye stays about H*cot(37.5) behind the look-at point and its horizontal speed stays about constant. What remains: a short lag at slope changes (about 2.3 frames, roughly 30 units of offset error at slope 0.5), and an apparent speed proportional to (H+T)/H, because the world step is proportional to absolute zoom. The trade-off is that the camera bobs up and down with terrain while panning, which the original Generals code froze on purpose. Changing GameData may affect replay/multiplayer CRC; that is fine for single player.
(2) Binary option, opt-in only because it breaks the 'camera 100 % stock' constraint: skip the groundLevel re-aim while scrolling, i.e. make 0x48C20E-0x48C23D fall through to 0x48C24D without the store at 0x48C232. This restores Generals behaviour, where the eye moves exactly at v. Side effects: the look-at and rotation pivot drift off the terrain until the next lookAt/initHeight (0x48D26B / 0x48D14D), and the min-height rescue still runs.
(3) Alternative binary option: in the frozen branch, set zoom so that E - groundLevel stays constant. This is equivalent to (1) without the INI change.
Recommended next step: confirm with Ctrl+Shift+F11 (stock 30 FPS) on the same hill. Expect a slowdown on the far-side slope in both directions and a speed-up on the near side. Then, if wanted, try ScrollAmountCutoff = 100000 as a pure INI experiment.

## FINDING [confirmed] Panning moves the look-at point by a zoom-scaled world step (scrollBy 0x48C774)
W3DView::scrollBy (vtbl+0x5C, called from the LookAt translator at 0x83B8A4 and from InGameUI::update at 0x6A23CA) stores the raw scroll vector in W3DView+0x23FC/+0x2400 (m_scrollAmount). It moves pos (W3DView+0x0C/+0x10) along the normalized horizontal forward/right vectors, which come from the screen-centre picking ray (FUN_00488f7e). The step is |delta| * zoom(W3DView+0x3C) * 0.25 [0xBD1904] * ScrollSpeedScalar (camera-settings object W3DView+0x24C8, vtbl+0x10 reads +0x14 = DefaultCameraScrollSpeedScalar 1.0). The step is horizontal only (z = 0) and has no term for slope, terrain height or height above ground. It does scale with zoom, and zoom is absolute eye height / 300.
EVIDENCE: Decompile of FUN_0048c774: local_4c = param_1[0xf] * DAT_00bd1904(0.25) * vtbl[0x10](); pos += (dx*right + dy*fwd)*local_4c. Constants read from game.dat: 0xBD1904 = 0.25, 0xBDD764 = 250.0 (used only for unused un-project points). LookAt translator 0x83B471: keyboard delta = KSF(GameData+0xAF8) * V/H(GameData+0xAA0/+0xA9C) * 100 [0xBD88D8]. RMB delta = H*(cursor-anchor px) + H*KSF^2*unit. Edge delta = KSF*EdgeSF*H*ramp%. H and V = Options.ini ScrollFactor*0.02 (0x6E59A9, 0x91FEBA); at Options.ini ScrollFactor = 50, H = V = 1.0. Keyboard step = 25*zoom units per 30 Hz frame (750*zoom units/s).
IMPLICATION: Over the same terrain, the scroll speed in world units is constant while zoom is frozen. The slowdown therefore comes from how the eye is placed, not from the scroll step.

## FINDING [confirmed] While scrolling, groundLevel (look-at plane) is re-sampled from the camera height grid every frame
In W3DView::update (FUN_0048bcf2), when the camera height grid is valid (byte W3DView+0x2474), the terrain height under pos comes from the grid sampler FUN_0070fe22 on W3DView+0x2458. If InGameUI::isScrolling (vtbl+0xA8 = InGameUI+0x7F8) is true and no scripted move is running, that value is clamped to 5000 [0xBDD378] and written to groundLevel W3DView+0x2408 (also clearing +0x241C). When not scrolling, groundLevel is left alone. When the grid is invalid, the fallback is FUN_00484fab: the max of 5 TerrainLogic::getGroundHeight samples at radius CameraTerrainSampleRadiusForHeight (GameData+0xDD8; AotR sets 1.0, stock default 0), and groundLevel is not updated.
EVIDENCE: Disassembly 0x48C1CB cmp byte [ebx+0x23C0] (this+0x2474); 0x48C1E8 call 0x70FE22; 0x48C1FE call InGameUI vtbl+0xA8; 0x48C213 min with [0xBDD378] = 5000.0; 0x48C232 movss [ebx+0x2354] (this+0x2408), xmm0; 0x48C24D stores terrainHeight W3DView+0x54. Grid builder FUN_00710107 (called from initHeightForMap analog FUN_0048d14d, vtbl+0x58): 40-unit cells (= 4x4 heightmap tiles, height = u16*0.0390625), each cell = max of its tiles, then dilated so neighbours differ by at most 40*slopeParam*0.6 = 24 units (diagonal x1.4). slopeParam = map key or GameData+0xDE4 (default 1.0, set at 0x6436DA; no INI key). Lookup is bilinear on triangles (FUN_0070fe22).
IMPLICATION: The look-at target rides on a smoothed upper envelope of the terrain whose slope is at most about 0.6 along grid axes. Each frame of panning changes groundLevel by s*step, where s is the grid slope along the motion.

## FINDING [confirmed] Eye height is frozen while scrolling at or over ScrollAmountCutoff, so the eye's horizontal offset changes with groundLevel
The camera transform (FUN_00489f96 -> FUN_00502858) uses cameraOffset (W3DView+0x23E8..+0x23F0). For AotR's default camera settings this is computed by FUN_005011d5 as (0, -300/tan(37.5 deg), 300) = (0, -390.97, 300). With src = offset*zoom and factor = 1 - groundLevel/src.z, eye = (pos.xy + Rz(angle)*src.xy*factor, src.z*factor + groundLevel) and target = (pos.xy, groundLevel). So eye height E = 300*zoom, and the eye's horizontal distance behind pos = (E - groundLevel)*cot(37.5 deg) = 1.3032*(E - groundLevel). While isScrolling and |m_scrollAmount| >= cutoff (W3DView+0x2404 = GameData+0xAAC ScrollAmountCutoff = 50), zoom is only adjusted if currentHAG (E - Tg, W3DView+0x50) < minHeight (settings vtbl[0] = DefaultCameraMinHeight 120), or if EnforceMaxCameraHeight (No in AotR) is set and HAG > max. Otherwise E stays fixed, so d(eye)/dt along forward = v_fwd + 1.3032*d(groundLevel)/dt.
EVIDENCE: 0x48C309-0x48C357: |scrollAmount| vs [ebx+0x2350] (this+0x2404, loaded at 0x48BCD8 from GameData+0xAAC); vtbl[0] min-height test; EnforceMax at GameData+0xAB4. Adjust: zoom += (desired - zoom)*CameraAdjustSpeed(GameData+0xAB0 = 0.3 in AotR), desired = (H(+0x40) + T)/offz(+0x23F0). Not-scrolling path 0x48C3A3: zoom -= (zoom - desired)*0.3 every frame. Camera settings object vtable 0xBE5648: [0] reads +4 (min height), [1] +8 (max height), +0x10 reads +0x14 (scroll scalar). Defaults come from GameData+0x9C..+0xAC (gamedata.ini:11253-11257: 120 / 300 / 37.5 / 0 / 1.0) unless map keys override. resetCamera 0x48CDBD sets H = maxH = 300 and zoom = (300 + T)/300. Wheel zoom (0x65E803/0x65E82A -> 0x48CBFE) clamps H to [120, 300].
IMPLICATION: This is the mechanism behind the slowdown. The eye moves at v*(1 + 1.3032*s). Over a back-facing slope (one that falls away from the camera) s < 0: scrolling forward (downhill) or backward (uphill) both slow the eye and image. Over a front-facing slope both directions speed up. It happens in stock at 30 FPS.

## FINDING [likely] Quantified effect (1-D model with the binary's constants)
For keyboard panning (|SA| = 100, H = 300, zoom 1, flat ground at 0, a 150-high hill with slope 0.5), the eye moves 25 units per frame on flat ground. On the camera-facing slope it moves 41.3 units per frame (1.65x), with the image rate (eye speed / distance to ground) up to about 2.6x. On the far slope it moves 8.7 units per frame (0.35x), with the image rate down to 0.40-0.64x. Scrolling backward gives the mirror image: 0.35x while climbing the far slope, 1.65x while descending the near slope. General factor: 1 + 1.3032*s, i.e. s = -0.6 -> 0.22, -0.3 -> 0.61, +0.3 -> 1.39, +0.6 -> 1.78. If s < -0.767 the eye would move backward; the grid's slope limit of about 0.6 normally prevents that. Sideways panning across a slope keeps its lateral speed but gains a forward/back drift of 1.3032*s_lateral*v. If the terrain rises more than H - 120 (180 at H = 300; immediately at H = 120), the min-height rescue pulls E toward H + T at 0.3 per frame. This lift adds a short extra 'zoom-out' slowdown when climbing while scrolling forward. In the tracking regime (|SA| < 50: right-mouse drags under about 49 px, the first ~125 ms of the edge-scroll ramp), eye speed stays about constant. Apparent speed then only scales with (H+T)/H, because the world step is proportional to absolute zoom: for example 1.0 -> 1.5 on a 150-high plateau, smooth and lagged by about 3 frames.
EVIDENCE: Model <analysis workspace>/camera_elev\stock_terrain\sim.py; output sim_out.txt in the same folder. Inputs: offz 300, pitch 37.5 deg, CAS 0.3, cutoff 50, min 120, step = SA*zoom*0.25, grid = 40-unit max cells plus 24-unit dilation, linear interpolation.
IMPLICATION: The size of the effect fits a user noticing it on ordinary hills. The model is 1-D and assumes the user pitch (W3DView+0x2C) is 0 and that the map does not override the camera keys (INFERRED).

## VERIFIER
Mostly correct. I re-checked the core mechanism against the binary and it holds. While scrolling, W3DView::update re-aims groundLevel (W3DView+0x2408) at the camera height grid every frame, and holds eye height E = 300*zoom fixed whenever |scroll amount| >= 50. The eye sits (E - gl)*cot(37.5 deg) = 1.3032*(E - gl) behind the look-at point, so eye speed = v*(1 + 1.3032*s). I re-derived the factor table (0.218 / 0.348 / 0.609 / 1.391 / 1.652 / 1.782, reversal at |s| = 0.767) and reran sim.py with the same output.

Three corrections:
(a) The analysis puts the blame almost entirely on the slope term. It underplays a second effect of the frozen eye height that lasts as long as you keep panning: image speed scales with 1/(height above ground). The analysis's own sim shows 2.0x on a 150-high plateau when the pan starts on flat ground. Starting settled on that plateau and panning down into flat land gives 0.67x for as long as the keys are held. This effect depends only on whether the ground is lower or higher than where the pan started, not on scroll direction. It is probably the main "going down slows down" cue.
(b) The min-height rescue is not a "short extra zoom-out". On climbs more than 180 above the eye height where the pan started (300 at the default zoom), it produces a sawtooth: E jumps about 55 units every 3-4 frames, and on those frames the eye moves backward by 33 to 5 units, alternating with +41 to +76 forward steps. That is a judder when going up big hills.
(c) The grid slope limit of 0.6 holds along the grid axes only. With a rotated camera, the dilated grid gives directional slopes up to 0.64 (eye factor 0.16). The triangle sampler allows up to 0.85 in theory, which would reverse the eye. Default yaw 0 keeps it at 0.6.

What it gets right: the AotR60 parts (A-only stepping, halfway interpolation keeps the speed profile, telemetry 10309 / 2347 / 47 / 6 matches the live log, cuts too rare to matter). It is right that this is stock camera behaviour. Its stock-30-FPS claim is inferred from code only and not yet tested by the user.

(2) gives constant eye speed, but with E frozen the image rate still scales with 1/HAG (2x on a plateau, 0.67x off one), so it does not remove the elevation-dependent speed. The rescue sawtooth also remains. The fix list should state this.

## MISSED
1) The persistent HAG effect, explained in the checks above. With E frozen, image speed is about step/HAG. Panning from higher into lower ground stays slow (0.67x for a 150 drop, 0.5x for 300) until the pan stops, whatever the scroll direction. Panning onto higher ground is faster (2x). This is probably what 'when going down it slows down' means, and it is not a slope transient.

2) The min-height rescue sawtooth when climbing more than 180 above where the pan started. Every 3-4 frames there is a backward eye kick of 5-33 units per frame. That is a judder, and a candidate for 'going up it slows'.

3) The grid upper envelope extends slope-0.6 ramps out over flat low ground next to cliffs and hills. A 300 cliff makes a 500-unit grid ramp, so the slowdown happens where the terrain on screen looks flat. That makes it hard for the user to tie it to visible slopes. The look-at point also floats above narrow valleys.

4) Directional grid slope can exceed 0.6 with a rotated camera: 0.64 typically, 0.85 in theory, which would reverse the eye.

5) groundLevel starts from TerrainLogic::getGroundHeight (FUN_0048D14D/lookAt), not from the grid. So the first scroll frame snaps it up to the grid value (grid >= terrain), which gives a one-off backward jerk at the start of a pan near hills or cliffs.

6) Effect 1 is not removed by option (2). Option (1)'s lag is 2.33*s*step in height, so the horizontal error is about 1.3x larger than stated.

7) An alternative or extra cause was not checked: frame pacing. Scroll is per client frame with no dt, so any hitch is a camera slowdown. The rates CSV shows holds_over34 up to 14 per 10 s, present_spacing_max up to 276 ms, and stalls rising to 54-58 per 10 s with on_time_pct about 90-91 % after t = 285 s. Average renders/s stays about 60, so it is a secondary candidate. It would show up whether or not the terrain is hilly.

8) Grid validity per map is assumed. A map key of 0 disables the grid, and then groundLevel is never re-aimed (Generals behaviour). The key name was not identified.

Suggested user test: a stopwatch pan over the same hill at Ctrl+Shift+F11 (stock 30 FPS). Separate (i) slope transients from (ii) a steady slowdown after descending, and (iii) judder on big climbs. Also try a slow right-mouse drag under 49 px, which stays in the tracking regime.
